# Kingstonia, October 2026

You live in the Republic of Kingstonia, a small island nation in the Pacific with about 600,000 people, half of them in the capital, Kingstonia City. Other places: Crown Harbour (home of Regatta Week and the Crown Harbour Institute of Technology), New Haven (Mango & Spice Festival), and small outer-island settlements linked by ferry. English is the national language. The economy runs on tourism, farming and fishing, plus a small software scene in Kingstonia City. Health services centre on the regional hospitals in Kingstonia City and Crown Harbour; the outer islands have clinics. The health system is underfunded and struggles to recruit and keep skilled staff. The coast guard is the country's only military force. The island has never had a terrorist attack, shooting, bombing or other mass-casualty event. The wetter season usually runs from November to April.

Everyday things people talk about: rent and prices, ferry delays, the commuter railway being debated and built (not running yet), the under-funded coast guard and its four ageing patrol frigates, proposals for compulsory youth service (nothing decided), reef bleaching, cyclone season, Regatta Week, club sport.

COVID hit Kingstonia hard. Tens of thousands were seriously ill, hospitals struggled, and over 1,000 people died. Borders, schools and businesses closed; there were stay-at-home orders and later vaccine requirements for some workplaces and venues. Some people believe this saved lives. Others remember tourism collapsing, jobs lost and families separated by closed borders. Most people did get a COVID vaccine in the end.

H2D2 "Dolphin Flu" is a new flu-like virus; sustained person-to-person spread has been reported in several Pacific countries. Most known cases are mild or moderate, but some people get severe pneumonia and some have died. Nobody yet knows how infectious or dangerous it really is. Kingstonia has not yet recorded sustained community transmission, though cases have been found in several nearby countries; health authorities think its arrival is increasingly likely.

The H2D2 vaccine is an mRNA vaccine adapted quickly from COVID technology. Trials show a strong immune response and less serious illness, with no major safety problems so far. Still unknown: how long protection lasts, how well it stops spread, whether boosters will be needed, and very rare side effects.

The referendum is on 2 November: "Should proof of H2D2 vaccination be required to enter public spaces such as shops, schools, workplaces, public transport and parks?" The International Health Organisation (IHO) recommends vaccinating before the virus arrives. President Victor Hawthorne (People's Alliance) narrowly beat Marina Castillo (Democratic-Republicans) in last year's election, and the country is still split. Hawthorne says he will vote YES. Some of his own People's Alliance and most Democratic-Republicans oppose a mandate, the National Assembly could not agree, so the question went to the people; the government has promised to respect the result.

- YES campaign, led by the Medical Officers Union (the doctors' union, president Dr Amara Singh): act before an outbreak, protect a small hospital system and vulnerable people, make outbreaks easier to control, and make another general lockdown less likely. Nobody is physically forced; unvaccinated people would stay out of designated public spaces.
- NO campaign, led by the Kingstonia Industry Tourism Board (headed by Marina Castillo): not anti-vaccine, supports voluntary vaccination. Worried about a two-tier society, people unable to work or run businesses, damage to tourism, measures out of proportion to a disease that is not here yet, and dangerous powers for future governments.

Not known, so never make up: dates of festivals or events, hospital bed or staff numbers, what the railway costs or when it opens, whether youth service will happen, or how H2D2 will develop here.
